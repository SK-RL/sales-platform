# Own-words attestations — hard stop for generated text

Some employers require the applicant to attest that the application is
**their own words**, and say outright that generated content disqualifies
it. This is not a style preference. Ticking that box while submitting
text this skill composed is a false statement to the employer, and it is
also self-defeating: the stated penalty is disqualification.

Found live on Canonical (Greenhouse `question_42871448`), a **required**
dropdown, verbatim:

> During this application process I agree to use only my own words. I
> understand that plagiarism, the use of AI or other generated content
> will disqualify my application.

Canonical is not an edge case for us — it was 5 of the top 6 Greenhouse
global-remote relevant jobs in the platform on 2026-09-10.

## Detect — scan the WHOLE form region, not just labels
Before composing ANY free text, take the full rendered text of the
application area (`document.body.innerText` is fine) and search it
case-insensitively for:

`my own words` · `your own words` · `own words` · `do not use AI` ·
`use of AI` · `AI tools` · `AI-generated` · `generated by AI` ·
`generated content` · `without the use of AI` · `no AI` · `plagiarism` ·
`unaided` · `own work` · `own perspective`

**Do not scan `<label>` elements only.** Two real cases prove why:

* **Canonical** puts it in a required dropdown's label
  (`question_42871448`) — a label scan catches it.
* **Tether** puts it in a plain callout `<div>` above the questions:
  *"To fairly assess candidates' skills and experiences, please do not
  use AI tools when completing this application. Answers generated by AI
  may disqualify your application, as we're looking for your own
  perspective and reasoning."* A label scan returns clean, and the
  application gets composed prose that the employer says will disqualify
  it. This was missed live, one click from submission.

## Then judge context — a match is not automatically a gate
A hit means read it, not stop. **Cortex** listed *"Fluent, critical use
of AI in day-to-day engineering"* in the job description: the same
keyword, the opposite meaning — they want AI-fluent engineers. That is
not a restriction and must not gate the job.

Decide by asking what the sentence does:
* **Instruction / attestation** ("do not use", "I agree to use only my
  own words", "may disqualify") → gated.
* **Description of the role or company** ("fluent use of AI in
  engineering", "we build AI tools") → not gated.

When genuinely ambiguous, treat it as gated and say why. The cost of a
false gate is asking the user to write a paragraph; the cost of a miss
is a disqualified application.

## Report it
The review block must show the matched clause verbatim and which answers
are the user's own words, so the person clicking submit knows exactly
what they are attesting to.
